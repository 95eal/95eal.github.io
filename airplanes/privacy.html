Airplanes Multiplayer: privacy policy

Effective date: 21 November 2026

This policy is published at https://95eal.com/airplanes/privacy.html

Who we are

Airplanes Multiplayer is a Battleship-style game for Android in which you find and hit the head of an opponent's plane. The game is made and published by 95eal studio, the trading name of an individual developer, Silviu Buligan. That developer decides what data the game handles and why, and is the controller of the data described here, except the data other companies control themselves (see Data other companies control). For anything in this policy, write to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com.

The game is for people aged 13 and over. See Children.

The short version

  • There is no sign-up and you type nothing about yourself. The game signs you in to Unity anonymously. On Android, if Google Play Games has signed you in, the game links your Play Games profile to that account and saves your progress to Unity Cloud Save (see Google Play Games and cloud save). Your name in the game is drawn for you from fixed lists. You cannot type a name.
  • Your country flag and your platform are shown to other players only if you switch them on in Settings. Both start off.
  • The game shows ads (through Unity LevelPlay, with Google AdMob and Unity Ads) and sells optional items through Google Play. Ads use your phone's advertising ID.
  • Where the law requires it (the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland) you are asked for your choices first. On Android, one message from Google asks for both your ad choices and your analytics choice; on Windows, the game asks its own analytics question.
  • The game never sees your payment card or other payment details. Google handles payment.
  • Settings > Delete my data removes your entries from the live leaderboards, deletes your progress saved with Google Play Games, deletes your Unity account, asks Unity to delete your analytics data and wipes your game profile from your phone. Where Google's consent form applies (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), it then opens it again so you choose afresh.

What the game keeps on your phone

The game keeps three files in its own storage area on the phone. The main one is your profile (profile.json), with a backup copy of it if the file is ever found damaged. It holds:

  • your drawn name (stored as two numbers that pick words from the game's lists), and the country you chose, if you chose one;
  • whether Show my flag and Show my platform are on (both off until you switch them on);
  • your rating and division, match statistics, win streaks, achievements and difficulty progress, and the ratings of the computer opponents you have met;
  • your settings: sound and music volume, vibration, language, reduced motion, default difficulty, aeroplane model and colour;
  • which hints you have seen;
  • your analytics choice: whether it is on, and whether you have answered the question;
  • the ids of the items you have bought, copied from Google Play so the game works offline (for example, airplanes.remove_ads), and how many rewarded ads you have watched toward each item;
  • an ad-timing clock: how long the game has been in the foreground, when the last ad was shown on that clock, and how many ads you have watched in total;
  • your daily streak, the items you have seen marked as new, and your bonus experience points;
  • Google Play Games saving: whether this phone saves your progress, whether the automatic save is turned off, whether you have answered the offer to save, and, while it saves, your Play Games player id, which the game uses only to check that Play Games is still signed in to the same player and sends nowhere (Android's own backup holds it with the rest of the profile);
  • a list of the achievements already sent to Google Play Games, so each is sent once; it is emptied if a different Play Games player is linked.

The profile holds no free text, no device model or name, and no Unity player id; the one id it can hold is the Play Games player id above. It is not encrypted and can be edited by someone with access to the phone's files, so your rating is a local display value.

Beside the profile are two small files:

  • resume.json, only while a friend match (Play with a friend) is on: the match's session and match ids, your seat and the host's, your opponent's drawn name (as two numbers), the time of your last contact with your opponent and how long the match waits for a player to come back, so that the game can take you back to the match if Android closes it. It is deleted when the match ends, and expires by itself soon after.
  • device.json: what belongs to the phone rather than to you: the graphics level, whether you or the game chose it, and how many times the game has measured the phone; and two flags, that the automatic save to Google Play Games is off on this phone and, on Android, that Google's privacy form is owed after Delete my data. It names no device.

Neither of the two is included in Android's backup.

The Unity services below and Google's consent component also keep small items of their own on the phone, such as your sign-in session, the last settings the game fetched, and your ad-consent choices.

Uninstalling the game removes what it keeps on the phone. On Android, the system's backup feature (on by default, and not something the game controls) can restore the profile together with the sign-in if you reinstall, so that your profile and your player come back as a pair.

What the game sends, and to whom

To other players

  • To your opponent, once per online or LAN match: your drawn name, your displayed rating, and your real rating (which their game uses to work out the expected result and shows nowhere); and how your plane looks (its model, colours, finish, intro and aiming mark) with your level, division, best league and Top x % standing. Your country is sent only while Show my flag is on, and your platform only while Show my platform is on.
  • To your local network, while you host a LAN game: your drawn name, so nearby players can find your game. This travels unencrypted on your local network.
  • While you search for a ranked match: the game you host or look for on Unity's matchmaking service carries your real rating, the range of ratings it accepts and a short matching word, with no name or id, so that you are paired with a player of similar skill. Other searching players' games read it; it is removed when the search ends.
  • To the leaderboards, after each ranked match that counts. See The leaderboards.

Nothing else about you reaches another player. The country your phone suggests when you open the country picker is not stored or sent anywhere until you choose it. There is no chat and no free text between players.

Computer opponents in ranked play. Some ranked opponents are the game's own computer players. A stand-in is played by your own phone, under a name, flag, platform and look the game makes up for it: no other player's device takes part and nothing about you goes to another player, and it moves your rating as any ranked match does. The ladder bots are copies of the game that the studio runs on its own computers. They search for and play ranked matches as anyone does, so a ladder bot you meet receives what any opponent receives (above). What a ladder bot records of a match holds no name, id, country or platform of either player, and it sends no analytics.

Google Play Games and cloud save

On Android, the game saves your progress with Google Play Games, so that any device signed in to the same Google account (another phone, a reinstall, or Google Play Games for PC) gets it back.

Google's Play Games software is part of the game and starts with it: if you have a Play Games profile, Google signs you in to it when the game starts, and the game cannot switch that off. Once Google has signed you in, the game links your Play Games profile to your Unity account and starts saving your progress by itself, with no press. If your Google account already holds progress and this phone has not played a ranked match, the game takes that progress; if both hold progress, it asks you which to keep, and never overwrites either without asking. If Google has not signed you in, nothing is saved until you press Save my progress under Google Play Games in Settings.

  • To Google: what Google's own Play Games software collects when it signs you in, under Google's policy: Google Play Games receives your play and shows it in Google's apps under your Play Games profile. The game also asks it for a one-time code that proves your Play Games sign-in to Unity. The game's achievements: when you earn one, or at launch for any you have earned that were not sent yet, the game tells Google Play Games which achievement you unlocked, or how far you have got on one that counts up. Nothing else goes with it. This happens only while the game saves your progress with Google Play Games, and only to the Play Games account linked to it.
  • To Unity Authentication: that one-time code. With it, Unity links your Play Games identity to your Unity account and keeps it on the account. On a fresh install the code is first used only to look for an earlier link; if there is none, Unity keeps nothing.
  • To Unity Cloud Save: a copy of your profile (the items listed under What the game keeps on your phone), without your Play Games player id, updated as you play.
  • To other players: nothing new. Your Play Games name is shown only to you, in Settings. Your name in the game stays the one drawn for you, on your plate, on the leaderboards, in analytics and in logs. Nothing from Play Games goes to the ad networks or to analytics.

Delete my data deletes the copy in Cloud Save and your Unity account with its link (see Deleting your data); your Play Games profile is Google's to delete.

Analytics

Analytics is optional, and the game plays exactly the same with it off.

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland (Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, the Czech Republic, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland and the United Kingdom), you are asked first, and where you are asked, nothing is collected for analytics until you agree:
    • On Android, the question is part of Google's consent message (below). Its App purposes (storing and reading information on your device, measuring content performance, understanding audiences through statistics, and developing and improving the game) are the studio's own use of your data for analytics. Analytics is on only if you consent to all four: Consent on the message's first page does, and so does switching all four on under Manage options. Do not consent leaves analytics off. On Android, Google's consent component, not the game, decides whether you are asked. If Google does not ask you, analytics is on by default, as everywhere else, and you can switch it off in Settings. If Google's component cannot answer (for example, when you are offline), analytics stays off unless you have already chosen.
    • On Windows, the game asks its own question. Allow and No thanks are equal in size and weight, and Back counts as No thanks. If the game cannot tell where you are, it asks.
  • Everywhere else, analytics is on by default and you can switch it off at any time in Settings.
  • Your answer follows you: once you have chosen, the game keeps your choice wherever you are.
  • Switching analytics off stops collection from then on. What was already sent is deleted only through Delete my data (below) or by writing to us.

The nine game events and everything they carry:

  • a match started: the mode (single player, LAN or online), the kind of match (single player, LAN, a game with a friend or ranked), the kind of opponent (the computer, a person, a stand-in, or one of the game's own bot players on the ranked ladder), the difficulty tier in single player, the board's width and height, and the number of planes;
  • a match ended: the mode, the kind of match, the kind of opponent, the result (win, loss, you left, your opponent left, or the connection was lost), the number of shots you took, how efficiently you searched and how well you chose your shots (each 0 to 1, only for a match played to the end), how long the match took in seconds, the difficulty tier in single player, and, in LAN and online matches, how many times you were away from the match (for example, in another app) and how many of those times you came back in time;
  • a mode chosen from the main menu;
  • a ranked search ended: how it ended, how long it took, and which of the game's rules ended it;
  • a hint shown: which hint;
  • an error shown: which error and on which screen;
  • an achievement earned: which achievement, how many matches you had finished, and the mode, opponent kind and difficulty of the match that earned it;
  • a difficulty tier opened: which tier, which rule opened it, and how many matches you had finished;
  • a rating asked: that the game asked Google Play to show its rating card, or that you opened the game's store page from Support us or Settings to rate it, which of the three it was, and how many matches you had finished.

No event carries your name, your country, your platform, free text, or a device identifier chosen by the game. Unity's software adds its own pseudonymous installation and player ids.

Ads

The game shows a banner on the menus and the result screen, full-screen ads between matches (only when you tap to start a new match, never during a match, and not in your first 15 minutes of play), and rewarded ads that you choose to watch for a bonus.

Ads are served through Unity LevelPlay (the ad mediation service that was ironSource's), which asks ad networks to bid to show an ad. The networks the game uses are Google AdMob, through Google Bidding, and Unity Ads. To show and measure ads they receive your phone's advertising ID, device details and how you interact with ads, and may use them under their own policies, which are linked below. The game declares Android's advertising-ID permission for this. You can reset or delete the advertising ID, or opt out of ad personalisation, in your phone's settings.

No purchase or ad changes how a match is played, your rating or fairness. Buying Remove ads (or the Supporter pack) stops the banner and the ads between matches; the rewarded ads you choose to watch stay available.

Your consent choices

  • Google's consent message (Google User Messaging Platform, UMP). Each time the game starts, Google's component checks whether a consent message is required where you are, which Google decides (for the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland). If it is, the game shows Google's message before it loads any ad, and no ad loads until it has been answered and ads are allowed. Your choices are stored by Google's component on your phone.
  • The analytics question: on Android it is part of Google's message, as described above; on Windows the game asks its own question.
  • Settings > Privacy settings. Where Google says you must be able to change your ad choice, Settings shows a Privacy settings button that opens Google's form again, where you can change or withdraw consent. A withdrawal takes effect at once.
  • Settings > Analytics switches analytics on or off at any time. On Android, while Privacy settings is shown and holds your answer, you change analytics there instead, and this switch is hidden.
  • Settings > Show my flag and Show my platform decide whether your chosen country and your platform are shown to opponents and on the leaderboards. Both are off until you switch them on.

Nothing in the game's play is gated on consent. Declined or unanswered, every mode plays the same; only the ads differ.

Purchases

The game sells six optional one-time items (Remove ads, Custom games, a Supporter pack, and three upgrades to the Supporter pack for players who already own Remove ads, Custom games or both) through Google Play Billing, with Unity In-App Purchasing connecting the game to Google Play. Google takes the payment: the game never sees or stores your card or other payment details. If you bought the full game in an earlier version of Airplanes (2017–2018), the game restores it from your Google Play account, and it removes ads as Remove ads does.

What the game stores is the list of product ids you own, which it reads from Google Play each time it starts and caches in your profile so that your items work offline. If a purchase is refunded, the next start that can reach Google Play removes it from the cache. A purchase still pending with Google grants nothing until Google reports it bought. Your purchase history is kept by Google under its own policy.

The leaderboards

The leaderboards are public: anyone playing the game can read them. After each ranked match that counts, the game writes:

  • your drawn name (shown live from your Unity account's name);
  • your score: your displayed rating and division as one coded number;
  • details shown with it: your displayed rating, your league, your division and the number of ranked matches you have played, and your platform only while Show my platform is on;
  • on the win-streak leaderboards: your streak, league, division and the date of your best.

You appear on your country's leaderboard only while Show my flag is on. Your country is never stored as a field of the entry; being on a country's board is how it shows. Your hidden confidence value is never sent.

When you open the My country tab with your flag off, the game reads the leaderboard of the country you chose, so Unity receives that country's code as the name of the board to read. Nothing is written and you are not added to it. If you have not chosen a country, nothing is read.

The live leaderboards keep your latest score until your next ranked match replaces it. Each month's leaderboard is archived when the month ends, and the archives keep every entry. If you switch your flag off, new entries stop going to your country's leaderboard, but the entry already there stays until it is removed.

Deleting your data

Settings > Delete my data

From the main menu, open Settings and press Delete my data (it is not offered during a match). The game asks you to confirm: “Delete your data? This deletes your Unity account, the analytics already sent, your entries on the live leaderboards, your progress saved with Google Play Games and everything this game keeps on this device: your rating, achievements, name and settings. A past month's leaderboard keeps its score. It cannot be undone.” Where Google Play Games is not available, the words about Google Play Games are left out. It does the following, in this order:

  1. removes your entries from the live leaderboards, through a Unity Cloud Code function that deletes only the caller's own entries;
  2. deletes your progress saved with Google Play Games from Unity Cloud Save, after waiting for a save already on its way;
  3. deletes your Unity Authentication account, with its link to Google Play Games if it has one (signing in first if it has to, unless you never had one);
  4. switches analytics off and asks Unity Analytics to delete the data sent from your device;
  5. wipes your profile on the phone (rating, achievements, name, settings and the other items listed under What the game keeps on your phone), with resume.json and device.json, and records your analytics choice as unanswered, so you may be asked again. It then writes device.json again, holding only the flags that the automatic save to Google Play Games is off and, on Android, that Google's form is owed. Where Google's consent form applies (the EEA, the UK and Switzerland), it then opens it again so you choose afresh, and the answer you give there decides analytics.

Each step runs only once the one before it has finished, and each has a time limit, so it can take up to about a minute and a half. If a step fails (for example, when you are offline), the steps after it do not run and your profile on the phone is not wiped, but the steps already done are not undone. The game then says “Your data could not be deleted. Nothing was deleted. Check your connection and try again.”, or, if a step went through, “Your data could not be fully deleted. Try again to finish.”, and you can try again. The automatic save to Google Play Games is turned off on this phone as the deletion starts, and stays off whether or not it finishes. Afterwards the game starts as it did the first time, with a new drawn name and a new anonymous account, and it saves to Google Play Games again only when you press Save my progress.

What it does not do:

  • Archived months of the leaderboards keep their entries, because an archived month can be removed only as a whole. Write to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com with your name as the game showed it and we will tell you what can be removed. If you used Delete my data on a version older than 17 October 2026, your live entries may still be there; write to us and we will delete them.
  • The installation id that Unity's software keeps on the phone is not part of the account and is not erased by it.
  • Your purchases are Google's records and stay with Google Play; the game reads them again at the next start. Your ad-consent choices are stored by Google's component on your phone and ads data held by ad networks is theirs; use Privacy settings and your phone's advertising-ID settings, and the providers' policies, for those.
  • If you reinstall before Android's next backup, the system's backup can restore the deleted profile; the game then signs in as a new player.
  • What Google keeps: your Google Play Games profile, the achievements the game sent to it, and what Google's Play Games software collected, are Google's, and the phone stays signed in to Play Games (the game cannot sign you out of it). You can delete this game's data from your Play Games profile, or the whole profile, in the Play Store app under your Play Games profile's settings, or at play.google.com/games/profile; Google explains how in Delete your Play Games data or Play Games profile. That does not delete what the game keeps; Delete my data does.

Other ways

  • Uninstalling removes what the game keeps on the phone (see the backup note above).
  • For anything else, or if you cannot use the game, write to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com.

Feedback

If you choose Send feedback in the game's Settings, your own email app opens a message to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com with the game's version, your device model and your Android version filled in. Nothing is sent unless you send that email yourself. We use what you send only to answer you and to fix problems, and we delete it once it is no longer needed for that.

Legal bases (GDPR)

Where the EU or UK General Data Protection Regulation applies, the game relies on these legal bases:

  • Consent: for analytics in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, where, when you are asked, nothing is collected until you agree (on Android in Google's consent message, on Windows in the game's question); and for personalised ads, through Google's consent message. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings (Analytics, Privacy settings); withdrawing does not affect what was done before.
  • Legitimate interests: for analytics everywhere else, to learn how the game is played and fix what goes wrong, with the opt-out in Settings > Analytics. You can object at any time by switching it off.
  • Legitimate interests also cover answering the feedback emails you send us and fixing the problems they report.
  • Contract: to provide what you use in the game: your Unity account, online play, the leaderboards and the items you buy.
  • Legal obligation: where the law requires us to keep or hand over data, for example tax and accounting records of sales, which Google Play keeps for purchases.

Your rights

Under the GDPR, and similar laws where you live, you have the right to:

  • access the data held about you;
  • rectification: have wrong data corrected;
  • erasure: have your data deleted (see Deleting your data);
  • object to processing based on legitimate interests, such as analytics outside the opt-in regions;
  • portability: receive the data you gave in a common machine-readable form;
  • withdraw consent at any time, as described above;
  • complain to a supervisory authority, in the country where you live or work, or in Romania, where the developer is: the National Supervisory Authority for Personal Data Processing (ANSPDCP), www.dataprotection.ro.

To use a right, write to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com. The game asks for no name or email address. If it saves your progress with Google Play Games, your Unity account is linked to your Play Games profile and your phone keeps your Play Games player id; otherwise it holds no direct identifier for you. Either way, to find your leaderboard entries we need the name the game showed. For data another company controls, ask that company (below).

Data other companies control

  • Consent choices (Google User Messaging Platform). Google's component stores your ad-consent choices on your phone. Change or withdraw them in the game at Settings > Privacy settings, when the button is shown. How Google uses consent data is set out in Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Ads (Unity LevelPlay, Unity Ads, Google AdMob). Unity and Google decide themselves how they use the ad data they receive, under their own policies, linked below. You control it through the game's Privacy settings, your phone's advertising-ID settings (reset it, delete it, or opt out of personalised ads), and the requests those policies describe.
  • Purchases (Google Play). Google keeps your payment details and purchase history in your Google account. See or manage them in the Play Store app under Payments & subscriptions, or at myaccount.google.com.

How long data is kept

  • On your phone: until you delete it with Delete my data or uninstall the game; after Delete my data, device.json keeps only the two flags described there.
  • Your Unity account, with its link to Google Play Games if it has one: until you delete it with Delete my data.
  • Your progress saved with Google Play Games (the copy in Unity Cloud Save): each save replaces the one before, and the copy is kept until you delete it with Delete my data.
  • Your Play Games profile: kept by Google under its own policy until you delete it (see Deleting your data).
  • Live leaderboard entries: your latest score, until your next ranked match replaces it, you use Delete my data, or we remove it on your request.
  • Leaderboard monthly archives: kept indefinitely; an archived month can be removed only as a whole.
  • Feedback emails: until they are no longer needed to answer you or fix the problem you reported.
  • Analytics, diagnostics, ads and purchase data held by Unity, Google and the ad networks: kept for the periods set by their own policies, which we have not stated here. Analytics data is deleted on request as described above.

Children

The game is not directed at children and is intended for people aged 13 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has used the game, write to contact@airplanesmultiplayer.com and we will delete what we can, as described above. In some countries the minimum age to consent to data processing is higher than 13; if that applies to you, please have a parent or guardian help with the choices the game asks you.
